01 / WHO HE WAS THE PERSON BEHIND THE VOICE

₊˚ ♫ ⋆｡ ♪ ₊˚ ♬.

A talented artist
gone too soon.

Jeffrey Scott Buckley was an American singer, songwriter and guitarist born in Anaheim, California.
His expressive voice, adventurous musicianship and emotionally open performances made him one of the most distinctive artists to emerge from New York's early-1990s music scene.

He was the son of folk musician Tim Buckley, but the two had little time together.
Jeff built his own musical identity, drawing on an ususually wide range of influences rather than following one genre or anyone else's expectations.

His debut album, Grace, arrived in 1994.
It was the only full-length studio album released during his lifetime.
His interpretation of Leonary Cohen's Hallelujah later introduced his music to an enormous new audience.

In May 1997, while preparing material for a second album in Memphis, Buckley drowned in Wolf River Harbor.
He was 30.
The recordings he left behind, including the unfinished material later collected as Sketches for My Sweetheart the Drunk, show an artist whose music was still changing.

02 / LIFE EVENTS A LIFE IN CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER

𓉸ྀི ⊹˙✧ ݁˖ ༘ ⋆｡˙

The tragic, beautiful journey of a legendary voice.

From a childhood surrounded by music to the East Village stages, the release of Grace, and the unfinished Memphis recordings.

1966
THE BEGINNING

Jeff Buckley is Born ℘ ࣪ ˖

Jeffrey Scott Buckley was born on November 17, 1966, in Anaheim, California, to Mary Guibert and singer-songwriter Tim Buckley. His family called him Scottie when he was young.

His parents separated when he was a baby, and he was raised primarily by his mother and stepfather, Ron Moorhead.

01
1970s
FAMILY & CHILDHOOD

A Complicated Connection to His Father ᥫ᭡.

Jeff had very little contact with his biological father, Tim Buckley. They met only briefly, when Jeff was a child. Tim died in 1975, aged 28.

Jeff grew up largely outside his father's musical world. Later, he was often asked about Tim, but he wanted to be recognised for his own work rather than treated as an extension of his father's career.

02
1970s-1980s
DISCOVERING MUSIC

Finding His Influences ˖ ݁♬⋆

Jeff became interested in guitar as a child and developed a broad musical taste. Led Zeppelin was an important early influence, alongside soul, blues, folk and musicians from many different traditions.

He studied at the Musicians Institute in Los Angeles and spent time playing in different bands and taking session work. He was initially more focused on guitar than on becoming a singer.

03
1990
A NEW CITY

Moving to New York ᯓ ✈︎

Jeff moved to New York in 1990. The Lower East Side and East Village gave him a place to explore experimental music, meet artists and develop his own performance style.

04
1991
A DEFINING PERFORMANCE

Greetings from Tim Buckley ˙⋆✮⋆˚࿔

Jeff performed at a tribute concert for his father at St. Ann's Church in Brooklyn. The performance brought attention to his extraordinary voice and helped introduce him to New York's music community.

It was an emotionally complicated occasion: Jeff had not grown up with a close relationship with Tim and he did not want his own career defined by his father's name.

05
1991-1992
COLLABORATION

Gods and Monsters with Gary Lucas ♪ ༘⋆

Jeff worked with guitarist Gary Lucas in the group Gods and Monsters. Their collaboration helped shape early versions of songs including Mojo Pin and Grace.

Lucas wrote musical pieces that Jeff developed with melodies and lyrics. Their work became an important part of the foundation for his later solo career.

06
1991-1993
LOVE & NEW YORK

Meeting Rebecca Moore ˙𓍢ִ໋ ᰔᩚ

Jeff met artist Rebecca Moore in 1991. They became partners, and their relationship was an important part of his early New York years and the emotional world surrounding the writing of Grace.

They later separated but remained connected. Their relationship is particularly relevant to understanding the intensely personal song Forget Her.

07
1992-1993
THE EAST VILLAGE YEARS

Performing at Café Sin-é ˙⋆☕︎₊˚⸝⸝

Jeff became closely associated with Sin-é, a small café in New York's East Village. Performing solo with his guitar, he played original songs alongside adventurous interpretations of music by artists he admired.

His sets could more from folk and blues to jazz, soul and unexpected covers. The intimate performances made it clear that he was a compelling live performer even without a full band.

08
1993
FIRST RELEASE

Live at Sin-é ‧₊˚♪ 𝄞⊹

Columbia Records released the four song Live at Sin-é EP in December 1993. It captured the stripped-back performances that had helped build Jeff's reputation.

09
1993-1994
RECORDING A DEBUT

Making Grace ♫⋆˚࿔

Jeff recorded Grace at Bearsville Studios in Woodstock, New York, with producer Andy Wallace and musicians including Mick Grøndahl and Matt Johnson. The sessions brought his solo songwriting together with a full-band sound.

The album combined seven original songs with three covers. Its arrangements moved between delicate guitar passages, dramatic strings, alternative rock and soulful vocal performances.

10
1994
THE BREAKTHROUGH

Grace is Released ᯓ 𝄞 ˎˊ˗

Released on August 23, 1994, Grace was Jeff's only full-length studio album released during his lifetime. It included Last Goodbye, Lover, You Should've Come Over, Grace, Dream Brother and his now-famous interpretation of Leonard Cohen's Hallelujah.

The album did not immediately make him a mainstream superstar, but it earned devoted listeners and critical admiration. Its reputation grew considerably in the years after his death.

A SONG LEFT OFF THE ORIGINAL ALBUM

The song of Forget Her

Forget Her was recorded during the Grace sessions and was considered for the album. The song was about Jeff's breakup with Rebecca Moore. According to reporting by the Los Angeles Times, Jeff later felt it was inappropriate to release after the pair reconciled. The song was left off the original 1994 tracklist and eventually appeared on the 2004 Grace: Legacy Edition.

That personal history helps explain why the song mattered so much to him: it was not simply another track, but something tied to a real relationship and a private period of his life.

11
1994-1995
LOVE & MUSIC

Elizabeth Fraser ❤︎₊ ⊹

During this period Jeff had a relationship with Elizabeth Fraser, singer of Cocteau Twins. The two recorded the unreleased duet All Flowers in Time Bend Towards the Sun.

Cocteau Twins' Rilkean Heart is identified on the band's official website as being about the late Jeff Buckley. The song first appeared on the 1995 Twinlights EP and was later revoked for Milk & Kisses. Fraser also spoke about the song's personal emotional territory in a 1996 interview, so it is best understood as a deeply personal reflection rather than a simple biography in song.

12
1994-1996
TOURING

Taking Grace around the world ⊹₊⟡⋆

Jeff and his band toured extensively across North America, Europe, Japan and Australia. The songs continued to evolve onstage, with performances that could be more expansive, spontaneous or intense than the studio recordings.

Grace earned a gold certification in France, and Jeff received the Grand Prix International du Disque from the Académie Charles Cros in 1995.

13
1994-1997
LOVE & PRIVATE LIFE

Joan Wasser ₊˚⊹ ᢉ𐭩

Jeff's relationship with musician Joan Wasser began in the mid-1990s. Wasser, who later became known as Joan as Police Woman, was his partner during his final years. Accounts have described them as engaged shortly before his death.

Joan was among the people closest to him during this period. Their relationship is part of the personal story of his final years, alongside his friendships and the musical work he was developing in Memphis.

14
1996-1997
A MUSICAL DIRECTION

The Memphis Recordings 𝄞⨾𓍢ִ໋

Jeff began working on a second album, recording with producer Tom Verlaine in New York and Memphis. The new material had a different, sometimes rougher sound from the carefully arranged Grace.

He continued writing and revising songs, experimenting with new arrangements and playing small, sometimes unannounced performances under different names. The album was still unfinished when he died.

15
May 29, 1997
MAY 29 · HIS FINAL DAY

Jeff Buckley Died Aged 30 ⊹﹏ ࣪ ˖

On the evening of May 29, 1997, Jeff went swimming fully clothed in Wolf River Harbor, a channel connected to the Mississippi River. A passing boat's wake pulled him under. His friend and roadie Keith Foti were nearby.

His death was ruled an accidental drowning. He had been in Memphis to work on his second album, and his death left that project unfinished.

A DETAIL FROM THE SEARCH

The Green Nail Polish

Jeff's body was recovered six days later, on June 4. By then, identification was difficult. A detailed account in At Length reports that his road manager, Gene Bowen, identified him using his navel ring and green nail polish on three toes.

The green polish is a documented part of the identification story. Some retellings connect it to his partner Joan Wasser, but the sources used for this archive do not firmly establish who applied it, so that detail is not presented here as a confirmed fact.

16
1998
POSTHUMOUS RELEASE

Sketches for My Sweetheart the Drunk 𝄞⋆ ݁𝄢 .

Released on May 26, 1998, the two-disc collection brought together recordings from Jeff's final creative period. It was assembled after his death from unfinished studio material and other recordings.

Tracks such as Everybody Here Wants You, Morning Theft and Vancouver reveal how his sound was developing beyond Grace.

17
2000s-today
A GROWING LEGACY

New Listeners Discover His Music .⋆⭒˚𖠋𖠋𖠋

Archival releases such as Mystery White Boy, the expanded Live at Sin-é, Grace: Legacy Edition and You and I have made more of Jeff's performances and recordings available to listeners.

His influence continues through his songwriting, adventurous covers, distinctive guitar work and the vulnerability of his performances.

THE LISTENING GUIDE

His Ten Most Popular Songs ࣪˖ ִ𐙚

"Music belongs in a place with hearts beating, and brains dreaming, and people falling in love." - 1995

01 · MOST STREAMED Lover, You Should've Come Over

Album: Grace (1994)Length: 6:43

"My body turns and yearns for a sleep that won't ever come"

A tender, aching song about love, regret, emotional growing pains and the feeling of being unready for a relationship until it may be too late. Its slow build mirrors the conflict between hope and heartbreak.

02 Hallelujah

Album: Grace (1994)Length: 6:54

"All I've ever learned from love was how to shoot somebody who outdrew you"

Jeff's interpretation of Leonard Cohen's song explores the complicated relationship between love, faith, desire and disappointment. Its quiet arrangement and fragile delivery make the performance feel intimate rather than triumphant.

03 Everybody Here Wants You

Album: Sketches for My Sweetheart the Drunk (1998)Length: 4:44

"I'm only here for this moment"

A warm, sensual soul song about admiration and the desire to make someone feel uniquely loved. Its relaxed groove and layered vocals show Jeff moving toward a smoother R&B-influenced sound.

04 Forget Her

Album: Grace (1994)Length: 5:12

"She was heartache from the moment that you met her"

Written in the aftermath of Jeff's breakup with Rebecca Moore, this song captures the contradiction of wanting to move on while remaining emotionally attached. Its personal history is also why Jeff ultimately withdrew it from the original Grace album after the relationship resumed.

05 Last Goodbye

Album: Grace (1994)Length: 4:36

"I hate to feel the love between us die"

An energetic but bittersweet farewell to a relationship. The bright, driving arrangement contrasts with the sadness of accepting that love cannot always make two people stay together.

06 Grace

Album: Grace (1994)Length: 5:22

"I'm not afraid, afraid to die"

One of Jeff's most dramatic compositions, building from a delicate opening into a powerful rock climax. Its imagery has often been read as a meditation on impermanence, change and the intensity of being alive.

07 So Real

Album: Grace (1994)Length: 4:43

"I love you, but I'm afraid to love you"

Co-written with Michael Tighe, So Real moves through dreamlike images, encertainty and emotional disorientation. Its unusual structure and surging arrangement make it one of the album's more restless songs.

08 I Know It's Over

Album: You and I (2016)Length: 7:02

"She needs you more than she loves you"

Jeff's intimate cover of The Smiths' song leans into its themes of loneliness, rejection and longing. Rather than copying the original, he gives it a quieter, exposed quality that makes the emotional weight feel very close.

09 Lilac Wine

Album: Grace (1994)Length: 4:32

"I drink much more than I ought to drink because it brings me back to you"

Originally written by James Shelton, the song uses the image of lilac wine to evoke intoxication, memory and romantic illusion. Jeff's delicate performance makes it feel suspended between a dream and a waking memory.

10 I Want Someone Badly

Album: Grace Legacy Edition · bonus disc (2004)Length: 2:36

"If you're leaving, just makes sure it's right"

Written by Nathan Larson, this short, direct performance centres on longing and the need for emotional connection. Its compact length and straightforward intensity make it a striking contrast to Jeff's longer, more elaborate songs.
